# Mexico: legal evidence snapshot

Source-corrected preliminary pilot · 0.2.0-audit.20261014.

The registry contains 45 selected IHR 2005 obligation units, 18 federal instruments, 116 domestic provision records and 86 obligation–provision correspondences. The actor inventory contains 18 entries. These counts describe documentary coverage, not a score of preparedness.

## What the targeted review changed

The SINAVE statutory mandate is located in General Health Law Article 133(II). Article 134 addresses surveillance, prevention and control of listed diseases. NOM-017 §§6.6.3–6.6.4 and 8.1.4 provide inter-level duties, reference the IHR and an international surveillance manual, and set a domestic immediate-notification chain. Those provisions must be followed before deciding that a procedure is absent.

Article 7 of the international health regulation provides a gratuity rule with exceptions, relevant to IHR Article 40. Articles 30 and 31 cover aircraft and maritime declarations. Their identification withdraws previous no-match or absence claims, without deciding full compatibility with the IHR.

## National IHR Authority

PAHO CD63/INF/1, paragraph 17, reports that Mexico had confirmed designation or establishment by 15 July 2026. The report does not name the entity or identify its domestic designation instrument. Those fields remain unresolved. DGE is separately identified for focal-point functions; do not infer it occupies the new Authority role.

## Limits

State laws, federal-state agreements, administrative practices and the full set of referenced manuals were not reviewed. The remaining historical annotations are preliminary. Current review labels overlap. Neither the old numerical gap counts nor the comparison of SPAR and NormTrace percentages is a validated finding of this version.
